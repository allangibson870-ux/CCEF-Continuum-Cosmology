# Is 3-D diamond the ground state of the grain geometry sector? (2026-10-12)

**Question** (from `CCEF_grain_Hamiltonian.md`): with H_Γ = Σ_v [U·sq(v) − K·hex(v) + W·θ(v)] at (K, U, W) = (1, 8, 4), annealing found locally-diamond 3-D space but never anything below diamond's −12 per junction. Is diamond the true ground state? Why is the dynamics glassy, and does quantum rewiring help?

**Code and outputs** (each has an `_output.txt`):

| Script | What it does |
|---|---|
| `ccef_geo_groundstate.py` | exhaustive "folded space" search |
| `ccef_geo_polytypes.py` | diamond vs lonsdaleite and other polytypes |
| `ccef_geo_dynamics.py` | classical vs simulated-quantum annealing |
| `ccef_geo_barriers.py` | energy landscape |
| `ccef_geo_ramp.py` | two-stage switch-on |
| `ccef_geo_seed.py` | seeded growth |

## 1. A rigorous bound [proved]
- With no 4-cycles, two 3-step paths from a junction v to the same point must leave v by different grains. So each third-shell point w is reached by k_w ≤ 4 paths, with Σ k_w = 36.
- Then hex(v) = Σ C(k_w, 2) and θ(v) = Σ max(0, k_w − 2).
- Per path, a pair (k = 2) earns ½ ring, while k = 3 or 4 earns less once W ≥ 2K.
- Hence **hex − (W/K)·θ ≤ 18 at every junction, so E/N ≥ −18** for all C4-free graphs.

Diamond (−12) lies strictly inside this bound, so the bound alone proves nothing about diamond.

## 2. The hypothesis as stated is refuted [computed, exhaustive within a family]
"Folded" 3-D spaces are the Haar graphs of every abelian group generated by three elements, Z³/L. Their local structure depends only on which of **73 short difference vectors** lie in L. Enumerating **every** lattice those vectors generate gives **3,415 distinct valid graphs**, and the energy improves steadily as space curls up:

| Shape of space | Best E/N (W = 4) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 3-D extended (diamond, L = 0) | −12 | unique in its class |
| 2-D slab (one direction closed) | −13 | L ∋ (0, 0, 3) |
| 1-D tube (two directions closed) | −15 | |
| **Finite tiny torus** | **−18** | Z³/3Z³: 54 junctions; straight zig-zag chains close after 6 steps |

- **38 graphs beat diamond at W = 4** (704 at W = 2.5), and all of them are compactified.
- The tiny torus **saturates the rigorous bound**: 0 squares, 18 rings, θ = 0.
- So the true ground state of H_Γ is a **crumpled gas of minimal closed universes**, not extended space. The annealing runs never found it only because the dynamics are glassy.
- **Diamond is metastable under the original rules.**

This is the grain-network version of the well-known **crumpled phase** of Euclidean dynamical triangulations (Ambjørn, Jurkiewicz, Loll and others).

## 3. A local fix that makes diamond provably a ground state [proved; uniqueness supported]
**The angle rule:** a pair of grains meeting at a junction may lie on at most 2 rings. Add V·Σ_angles max(0, load − 2) with V ≥ K.
- Each junction has 6 grain-pairs, so hex ≤ 12 and, for **any** graph, **E/N ≥ −12**. Diamond attains it. **Diamond is a ground state.**
- **Uniqueness, within the folded family (exhaustive):** with the rule, diamond is the *only* graph reaching −12.
- **Polytypes:** lonsdaleite (2H), 4H, 6H and stacking faults also have 12 rings and all angle loads equal to 2. But every hexagonally-stacked junction has θ = 1, so W makes them cost more:

| Structure | E/N |
|---|---|
| diamond 3C | −12.00 |
| 6H | −10.67 |
| 4H | −10.00 |
| lonsdaleite 2H | −8.00 |
| diamond with one fault | −11.33 |

- **Uniqueness in general (sketch, not a complete proof):** E/N = −12 forces, at every junction, no squares, θ = 0 and all six angle loads exactly 2. That pins the third shell to diamond's structure: each second-shell junction pairs with two other branches, each branch missing a different one. Diamond's cycle space is generated by its 6-rings, so a covering argument suggests every ground state is a **diamond space form**: infinite diamond, or a finite torus large enough that no short wrapping loops appear (e.g. the 216-junction 3×3×3 torus). Twisted quotients are expected too but were not checked.
  - **Intriguing:** this matches CCEF's finite-universe picture, where the ground states are finite flat 3-D universes, possibly twisted.
- **The rule plays the role causality plays in CDT:** a local constraint that removes the crumpled phase.

## 4. Glassy dynamics and quantum rewiring [computed]
**216 junctions, angle rule on; ground state −12:**

| Dynamics (≈10⁵ moves) | Final E/N | Perfect junctions | Shells (diamond: 4, 12, 24, 42) |
|---|---|---|---|
| classical annealing, 10 sweeps/T | −6.94 | 0% | 4, 12, 29, 55 |
| classical annealing, 30 sweeps/T | −7.32 | 0% | 4, 12, 29, 53 |
| **simulated quantum annealing** (8 Trotter replicas, link transverse field, same computing cost) | −6.73, −6.72 | 0% | 4, 12, 29, 56 |
| rule off then switched on late | −7.02, −6.98 | 0% | |
| rule ramped up gradually | −7.23, −7.34 | 0% | |
| seeded growth (half crystal) at T = 0.3–1.5 | −6.9 to −7.4 | seed absorbed | (inconclusive: seed too thin for this box) |

**Landscape:**
- **Diamond is very stable.** The cheapest single rewiring costs **36**, with a median of 96.
- **The frozen states have extended defects.** Of 1,425 single moves touching defects, only 1 went downhill. Of **8,498 two-move paths, none** went downhill.

**Reading:**
1. **The rule that makes 3-D the ground state also hides it.** Without the rule, annealing reaches locally exact diamond (≈ −11) by passing *through* over-ringed shapes. With the rule those shortcuts are forbidden, and runs freeze in a tangled, more tree-like glass (≈ −7).
2. **Quantum rewiring does not anneal the defects in this approximation.** Simulated quantum annealing at equal cost does slightly *worse*. With barriers of ~50 per move and defects needing many coordinated moves, tunnelling amplitude ~ (Γ/ΔE)ⁿ is hopeless, just as thermal hopping is.
3. **Caveats:**
   - Real-time unitary dynamics and the exact rewiring term were not simulated. The link transverse field is a standard proxy (Martoňák–Santoro–Tosatti), not Ĥ_move itself.
   - The runs cover ~10⁵ moves. Geometrogenesis may have astronomically more steps available, and the energy does improve slowly with cooling time (−6.94 → −7.32 for 3× longer).
   - Note that the rewiring moves are already non-local (any two grains anywhere).

## 4b. Seed growth in a bigger universe [computed]
**Code:** `ccef_geo_seed_big.py` → `ccef_geo_seed_big_output.txt`.

**Setup:**
- a 6×6×6 diamond torus with **1,728 junctions** (8× the earlier box);
- a slab 2 cells thick randomly rewired, leaving a crystal 4 cells thick (cells 3–4 fully perfect);
- angle rule on;
- fixed temperature, 60 sweeps (~10⁵ moves per run);
- the fraction of perfect junctions tracked cell by cell across the box.

| T | Energy E/N (start −7.03) | Perfect junctions, start → end | Profile by cell, end (start [0, 0, .50, 1, 1, .25]) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | → −8.49 | 45.8% → 43.9% | [0, 0, .44, 1.00, 1.00, .19] |
| 0.6 | → −8.38 | 45.8% → 41.6% | [0, 0, .44, .96, .96, .13] |
| 1.0 | → −8.37 | 45.8% → 41.8% | [0, 0, .44, 1.00, .92, .15] |
| 1.5 | → −8.36 | 45.8% → 39.9% | [0, 0, .35, .89, .94, .21] |

**Reading:**
- **The crystal–tangle front is pinned at every temperature.**
  - The tangle never converts into crystal: cells 0–1 stay at 0% perfect.
  - The crystal core survives, with slight erosion only at its edges (2–6%).
- The energy falls only because the tangle relaxes *within itself* toward its own glass.
- That happens despite a large driving force: about 3.6 energy units per junction separate crystal (−12) from the relaxed tangle.
- This matches the landscape analysis (§4): adding one more layer of crystal means rewiring the tangle into the crystal's exact 3-step registry. That is a many-move collective rearrangement with no downhill path one or two moves at a time.
- **So 3-D space cannot grow from a seed under local rewiring dynamics on these timescales.** Nucleation-and-growth fails like annealing from scratch.

The 216-junction test earlier was inconclusive because its seed was too thin. This one isn't, and the answer is the same.

## 4c. Collective "template" moves: space grows [computed]
**Code:** `ccef_geo_template_diag.py`, `ccef_geo_template.py` (outputs alongside).

**Template move (a growth rule, like epitaxy).** A junction on the crystal front, plus its 4 registry neighbours (a "star"), has all its grains rewired in one go (~8 degree-preserving swaps) so that it continues the crystal's registry. The whole composite move is accepted or rejected by Metropolis on the total energy change. The front is defined by registry: a junction is "in registry" if its 4 neighbours are where the crystal pattern puts them.

**How big must a move be before it pays?** (120 trials each, big seed)

| Template size | Rewirings | Median dE | Downhill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 junction | ~2 | 0 | 38% |
| **star (junction + 4 neighbours)** | ~8 | **−25** | **77%** |
| star + next layer | ~26 | −62 | 89% |

**Growth runs** (1,728-junction 6×6×6 universe; 5,000 collective moves interleaved with 15,000 single swaps; angle rule on):

| Run | Start | After ≤ 833 collective moves | Accepted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Big seed, T = 0.6, star templates | E/N −7.03, 46% perfect | **E/N = −12.000, 100% perfect: the exact ground state** | 174 |
| Big seed, T = 1.5, star templates | same | **−12.000, 100%** | 177 |
| **Tiny seed** (one cubic cell, 0.5% of junctions in registry), T = 0.6 | E/N −0.03, 0% perfect | **−12.000, 100%** | 527 |
| Control: **unguided** composite moves of the same size (8 random swaps near the front) | E/N −7.03, 46% perfect | −7.86, 43% perfect (slight erosion; no growth) | 76 |

**Reading:**
1. **Guided collective moves cross exactly the barriers that local moves cannot.** The whole universe converts to perfect 3-D diamond within a few hundred accepted moves, at both temperatures.
2. **A single one-cell seed converts the entire 1,728-junction universe.** Geometrogenesis as nucleation-and-growth works once the dynamics includes star-sized collective rewiring.
3. **Size alone does not do it.** Unguided moves of the same size fail. The guidance (continuing the existing pattern) is the essential ingredient.

**Caveats:**
- **The registry is taken from the seed's lattice** (a fixed labelling). Junctions are identical, so the particular labelling is not extra information. But it guarantees that growth fronts meeting around the closed universe **close up consistently**. With a purely local continuation rule, an incommensurate universe size would leave dislocations. Not tested.
- **This is a non-equilibrium growth rule, not an exact equilibrium simulation of Ĥ.**
- **A star template is an ~8-grain collective operator.** In Ĥ it would need to be a genuine collective rewiring term, since perturbative generation from single swaps is suppressed like (Γ/ΔE)⁷. That is a new requirement on the grain Hamiltonian **[OPEN]**.

**Intriguing (conjecture, not evidence):**
- Growth from **one seed** produces a **single crystal**: one registry everywhere, with no domain walls.
- That is the grain analogue of inflation's "one causal patch", and it would also fit the gentle, defect-free switch-on that the monopole argument needs.
- Several seeds would presumably give a polycrystal with domain walls (untested).

## 5. Conclusions
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is 3-D diamond the ground state of H_Γ as written? | **No (refuted).** Crumpled tiny tori reach −18 (the rigorous bound). Extended 3-D space is only metastable. |
| Can a local rule make it the ground state? | **Yes (proved).** The angle rule (≤ 2 rings per grain-pair) gives E/N ≥ −12 for every graph, attained by diamond. W excludes the polytypes. Uniqueness is exhaustive within the folded family; a general sketch is given. |
| Why glassy? | Extended defects: no downhill 1- or 2-move paths, with barriers ~36–100 per move. The rule that selects 3-D removes the dynamical shortcuts. |
| Does quantum rewiring anneal the defects? | **Not in the path-integral approximation tested.** It is equal or slightly worse at equal cost, as expected for wide, many-move barriers. |
| Can space grow from a seed? | **Not with local moves** (the front is pinned). **Yes with collective star-template moves:** even a one-cell seed converts a 1,728-junction universe into the exact ground state (−12.000, 100% perfect), while unguided moves of the same size fail |
| Open | (a) a local continuation rule without fixed labels, and closure on incommensurate universes; (b) where star-sized collective terms come from in Ĥ; (c) multiple seeds (domain walls); (d) real-time quantum dynamics |

**Intriguing (not evidence):**
- Under the original rules, **3-D space survives only because the dynamics is glassy**: the true ground state is crumpled.
- Under the corrected rules, 3-D space is the ground state, with **finite flat universes** among the allowed forms, but it can't easily be reached.
- Either way, how space forms (geometrogenesis) looks like a *kinetic* question, not just an energetic one.
