# CCEF — Does the induced Skyrme coefficient equal 9/(16 pi^2)?  NO.
**2026-06-22. No hand-fitting.** Honest result of the explicit one-loop (heat-kernel) computation.

## What was tested
Phase 2 conjectured B2 = N_pi^2/(16 pi^2) = 9/(16 pi^2) = 0.0570 (matched the proton to
0.1%). To upgrade [CONJECT]->[SOLID] we computed the actual one-loop INDUCED 4-derivative
term of the S^3 sigma model (Seeley-DeWitt a_2 coefficient), integrating out the z=2 modes.

## Result of the computation
Operator Delta = -D^2 - X (sigma-model fluctuations), S^3 unit curvature:
  X_ij = R_ikjl d_mu phi^k d^mu phi^l = G_ij S - T_ij ,  Omega_munu^ij = d_mu phi^i d_nu phi^j - (i<->j)
  Tr(X^2) = S^2 + TrT2 ,  Tr(Omega^2) = 2(S^2 - TrT2)
  a_2 = (1/16pi^2)[ (1/2)Tr(X^2) + (1/12)Tr(Omega^2) ] = (1/16pi^2)[ (2/3) S^2 + (1/3) TrT2 ]
  (S = dphi.dphi ;  TrT2 = (d_mu phi . d_nu phi)^2)

Three findings, each fatal to the clean "9":
  1. WRONG OPERATOR: induced term is a 2:1 mix of S^2 and TrT2, NOT the pure Skyrme
     combination (S^2 - TrT2). The heat kernel generates both invariants.
  2. LOGARITHMIC, not a pure number: coefficient carries ln(k_UV/k_sol) (the z=1
     marginal running), so "B2 = const = 9/16pi^2" is not even the right form.
  3. MISSES THE PROTON: feeding the genuine parameter-free induced coefficient
     (log set by B3 ~ 1e-6, ln ~ 5-6) into the Skyrmion gives
        E_sol * R_sol ~ 9 - 13   vs proton 4.06   => ~2-3x too heavy-for-its-size.
     Lowering the log requires larger B3, which re-introduces the bilaplacian mass
     penalty -> no window reaches 4.06.

## Verdict
B2 = 9/(16 pi^2) is NUMEROLOGY. The 0.1% match was coincidental. The DERIVED (induced)
Skyrme coupling has a different operator structure, a logarithmic coefficient, and yields
E*R ~ 9-13. The Phase-1/2 "proton reproduced" result held only when B2 was treated as a
FREE parameter tuned to 0.057; with B2 genuinely derived it lands ~0.02 in a mixed
operator and the proton is missed by a factor of ~2-3.

## Standing of the S^3 route after this
  - Still FAR better than S^2 (E*R ~ 9-13 vs S^2's 19-261; off by 2-3x not orders).
  - The induced-Skyrme MECHANISM is correct and parameter-free, but its predicted baryon
    is 2-3x too heavy-for-its-size at one loop.
  - [caveat] a_2 convention risk: the 2:1 ratio vs Gasser-Leutwyler 1:2 normalisation
    should be cross-checked; a careful redo could shift the O(1) factor. But mixed-operator
    + log + E*R~O(10) are robust and none gives a clean 9 or hits 4.06.

## Where this leaves the matter sector
The proton mass-radius is NOT reproduced parameter-free by the one-loop S^3 Skyrmion;
it is ~2-3x off. Honest options:
  (a) two-loop / resonance (vector-meson-like) contributions to the 4-derivative sector
      could lower E*R further -- but that is beyond one loop and not guaranteed;
  (b) accept S^3 gives a baryon 2-3x too heavy-for-its-size (a near-miss, not a hit);
  (c) the gravity/cosmology spine remains untouched throughout (Phase 0).
