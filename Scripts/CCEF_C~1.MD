# Is A3≈1e-3 (proton-required) compatible with gravity/cosmology?

Direct answer: **Yes for gravity/cosmology proper — but it pushes the Lorentz-violation
scale down to ~GeV, which is the real obstruction.** The sectors that fight over A3 are
the **baryon mass vs Lorentz invariance**, not baryon vs gravity. The gravity spine
survives either value of A3.

Everything in gravity/cosmology depends on A3 only **logarithmically** (Planck mass) or
only at **UV scales k~k_UV** (slip/dispersion corrections), which are far above all
observable/horizon scales (k≪1). So **A3=1e-3 leaves the gravity sector and the
cosmological observables essentially intact.** The graviton is still massless, healthy,
z=1. Strictly: the gravity/cosmology compatibility check **passes**.

## 2. The casualty: the z=2 Lorentz-violation scale
The matter dispersion ω²=A1k²+A3k⁴ violates Lorentz invariance with fraction
(A3/A1)k², order-1 at k_UV=√(A1/A3). Converting to physical units (proton radius sets
1 CCEF momentum ≈ 66 MeV; Λ≈hadronic):

| | A3=1e-6 | A3=1e-3 |
|---|---|---|
| order-1 LV scale (k_UV) | ~66 GeV | **~2 GeV** |
| LV fraction at 1 GeV | 2.3e-4 | **0.23** |
| LV fraction at 10 GeV | 2.3e-2 | 23 |

Observational LV bounds are ~1e-15…1e-20 at GeV–TeV. So:
- **A3=1e-3 gives ~0.2 Lorentz violation at 1 GeV — excluded by ~19 orders of magnitude.**
- Even A3=1e-6 gives ~2e-4 at 1 GeV — excluded by ~11 orders.

## 3. The structural tension (the real result)
A3 ties together two scales: the baryon mass and the Lorentz-violation scale.
- **Baryon mass** wants k_UV only ~7–25× the soliton scale ⇒ k_UV ~ few GeV ⇒ A3~1e-3.
- **Lorentz invariance** wants the z=2 LV scale astronomically high (near Planck) ⇒
  A3 ≪ 1e-6.

These pull in opposite directions and cannot both be satisfied by one A3. Gravity, being
log-insensitive, doesn't break the tie. **So the matter sector and Lorentz invariance are
in extreme tension — and reproducing the proton makes it ~1000× worse.**

## 4. The one hinge that could dissolve it: the a₂ prefactor
The conclusion "proton ⇒ A3~1e-3" rests on the derived B2(A3=1e-6)≈0.038. If the true a₂
prefactor is ~3× smaller (B2≈0.012 already at A3=1e-6), the proton is reproduced at
A3=1e-6 and there is **no new** baryon-induced LV tension — only the pre-existing generic
z=2 LV (order-1 at ~66 GeV, still a separate issue). My derivation (½·a₂, including the
0.949 anisotropy) gives 0.038; a factor-3 reduction is beyond plausible O(1) slack but
not impossible if the anisotropic a₂ geometry differs from the relativistic 2:1. **Pinning
the a₂ prefactor to better than ~2–3× is therefore the single number that decides whether
the proton costs A3=1e-3 (LV-excluded) or is free at A3=1e-6.**

## Honest verdict
- **Gravity/cosmology: compatible with A3=1e-3** (the explicit question — answer yes).
- **Lorentz invariance: not compatible** — A3=1e-3 puts order-1 LV at ~2 GeV, excluded.
- The genuine obstruction is **baryon-mass vs Lorentz-invariance**, linked by A3; gravity
  is a bystander (robust either way).
- Even the reference A3=1e-6 has a generic z=2 LV problem (~66 GeV) that the theory must
  address with a mechanism (emergent IR Lorentz invariance with a decoupled/Planckian LV
  scale, or SM particles as deeper composites with suppressed LV). If such a mechanism
  exists and also tolerates A3~1e-3, the baryon is saved; if it requires A3≪1e-6, the
  baryon is ~3× too heavy. The two outcomes hinge on (i) that LV-protection mechanism and
  (ii) the a₂ prefactor.

## What survives, cleanly
The gravity/cosmology spine — massless, healthy, z=1 composite graviton; logarithmic
Planck mass; intact slip/lensing predictions — is **robust across the entire A3 range**
and is not the locus of any tension. The open frontier is entirely: (a) the a₂ prefactor,
and (b) whether the z=2 UV completion can hide its Lorentz violation. Those two questions
now fully determine the matter sector.

## Caveats
- Physical-unit conversion uses proton radius → 1 CCEF momentum ≈ 66 MeV (Λ≈hadronic);
  the LV scale could shift by an O(1) calibration factor but stays in the GeV–100 GeV
  range — far below LV bounds either way.
- LV applies if observed SM particles are the n-field excitations with this dispersion;
  a compositeness/decoupling mechanism would change the bound.
- Graviton masslessness/health are structural (A3-independent), so robust.
