# Collective quantization of the S³ baryon (#2) — results

Quantize the stable frozen background (induced 2:1 operator, B2=0.02): rotational
inertia + breathing zero-point, projected to J=I=½. Computed from the relaxed profile
with the analytic-gradient machinery. Script run inline (uses `ccef_s3_dressed`/`relax3`).

## Background
M_cl = 24.18, R(energy-rms) = 0.364, **E·R = 8.79** (CCEF units).

## Rotational sector (isospin SU(2))
Moment of inertia (2-deriv + Skyrme-structure 4-deriv):
Λ = Λ₂ + Λ₄ = 0.406 + 0.464 = **0.870**.
Nucleon rotational energy E_rot = J(J+1)/(2Λ) = 3/(8Λ) = **+0.43 (1.8% of M_cl)**.
Implied N–Δ splitting 3/(2Λ) = 1.72 CCEF units ≈ 210 MeV (E₀=122.5 MeV) vs 293 MeV
physical — right order of magnitude, a mild success for the rotational sector.
**Sign: positive — it ADDS to the mass.**

## Breathing / radial zero-point
Derrick curvature E″ = 2E₄+6E_pot = 32.9; breathing inertia M_b = 2.73 ⇒ ω_b = 3.47,
zero-point ½ω_b = **+1.74 (7.2% of M_cl)**. (ω_b sits just below the 2-gap threshold
2Λ=3.77, so it is a genuine discrete breathing mode.) **Sign: positive — ADDS.**

## Net effect — the wrong way
| quantity | value | E·R |
|---|---|---|
| classical | M_cl=24.18 | 8.79 |
| + rotational (J=½) | +0.43 | 8.95 |
| + breathing zpe | +1.74 | **9.58** |

The cleanly-computable quantum corrections **raise** the mass by ~9% and push E·R from
8.8 to **9.6** — *away* from the proton's 4.06, not toward it. This is expected: both
the rotational energy and the vibrational zero-point are positive.

## Where the hoped reduction would have to come from
The only negative quantum contribution is the **Casimir / one-loop vacuum energy** (the
regularized sum of zero-point shifts of *all* fluctuation modes), which in Skyrme-type
models is typically −10% to −30% of M_cl. That is a separate, hard calculation (full
fluctuation spectrum + regularization) and was not done here. But the arithmetic is
unforgiving: starting from E·R≈8.8 (or 9.6 after the positive corrections), even a
generous −30% Casimir gives E·R ≈ 6–6.7 — **still well above 4.06**. Quantization
cannot bridge a factor of ~2 with corrections that are intrinsically ~10–30% (1/N_c).

## Verdict
Collective quantization, as the primary lever, **fails** to close the nucleon
mass-radius gap: its computable parts (rotation + breathing) make the baryon heavier,
and even the favourable Casimir term is too small to reach the proton. This is the
**third independent matter-sector lever to fail** (after core-melting and UV re-pin).

Honest standing of the matter sector: the CCEF S³ baryon is robustly **~2× too
heavy-for-its-size** (E·R≈9 classical, ≈9.6 quantized), and no parameter-free effect
computed so far closes it. The rotational sector does, encouragingly, reproduce the
N–Δ splitting to the right order. The gravity/cosmology spine (massless, healthy, z=1
composite graviton) remains untouched and solid.

## Honest implication
The ~2× baryon-mass tension looks structural, not a missing small correction. Options
that remain (all genuine work, none guaranteed): (i) a full Casimir computation to pin
its sign/size for this functional; (ii) multi-soliton/binding (resume #1) — but binding
is ~10–30%, same order, unlikely to bridge ×2; (iii) revisiting whether the S³ map and
the induced operator normalization (CC694B's a₂ 2:1 ratio, flagged there as
convention-risky) are right — an O(1) error there is the only thing that could move E·R
by a factor of ~2. That operator-normalization recheck is now the highest-leverage item,
above further quantum corrections.

## Caveats
- Λ₄ uses the standard Skyrme rotational structure; the induced 2:1 operator's exact
  rotational coupling could shift Λ by an O(1) factor (changes E_rot, already tiny).
- Breathing inertia is a scaling-mode proxy; ω_b good to O(1).
- Casimir vacuum energy not computed (the decisive negative term).
