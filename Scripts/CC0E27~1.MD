# #4 Principled B2(χ) from FRG  &  #5 UV re-pin — results

Scripts: `ccef_s3_frg.py` (FRG B2(χ) + frozen scan, analytic gradient verified 1e-10).
Induced 2:1 operator (CC694B). Couplings A1=1, A4=3.5553, Λ=1.886.

## #4 — Principled B2(χ): does NOT resolve the trade-off at one loop

**Derivation.** The positive, stabilizing induced Skyrme term (CC694B's a₂ heat-kernel
coefficient) is built from ∂φ, and for φ=χn its 4-derivative invariants scale as
S²,TrT2 ∝ χ⁴(∂n)⁴. The coefficient also carries a log whose IR cutoff is the *local*
fluctuation gap; in the broken phase that gap ∝ χ (mass ∝ amplitude), so the log grows
as χ→0. This gives the FRG-motivated, non-power-law form

    B2_eff(χ) = B2₀ · χ⁴ · (L₀ − ln χ)/L₀ ,   L₀ = ln(k_UV/gap) ≈ 5–6.

**Test (dynamical χ, c=m_σ/Λ=0.70):**

| 4-deriv form | E·R | χ_min | virial |
|---|---|---|---|
| power χ⁴ | 0.63 | 0.001 | 1.07 (collapsed) |
| power χ² | 1.61 | 0.001 | 1.10 (collapsed) |
| **FRG χ⁴·(L₀−lnχ)** | 0.95 | 0.001 | 1.07 (collapsed) |

The log enhancement helps a little (E·R 0.63→0.95) but the χ⁴ still wins in the deep
melt, so the stabilizer dies and the soliton **still collapses** (χ→0, non-stationary).
Separately, integrating out the amplitude (σ) fluctuation gives a *negative* (∝−χ²/m_σ²)
quartic — attractive, destabilizing — so that channel doesn't help either.

**Conclusion #4:** one-loop FRG/heat-kernel does **not** produce a melt-surviving
positive stabilizer; the trade-off (die-in-melt ⇒ collapse / survive-melt ⇒ heavy)
persists. Resolving it requires either (i) the CCEF **no-singularity axiom** as a
selection rule (χ>0 everywhere ⇒ pick the smooth partial-melt branch, via a constrained
solver), or (ii) genuinely non-perturbative FRG (a strong-coupling fixed-point B2(χ)
that does not vanish) — beyond one loop. Melting is not the lever.

## #5 — UV re-pin: the real lever, and it points the *other* way

**Frozen E·R vs B2₀** (no melting, χ=1):

| B2₀ | E·R |
|---|---|
| 0.008 | 4.80 |
| 0.015 | 6.99 |
| 0.020 | ~9 |
| 0.025 | 10.88 |
| 0.040 | 16.57 |

**Smaller** B2 lowers E·R (the resume's "larger stabilizer → lower E·R" was backwards).
The proton (E·R=4.06) needs B2₀≈0.007; E·R≈4.8 already at B2₀≈0.008. So the gap is
closable by the **correct (smaller) Skyrme coefficient with no melting at all** — if the
UV-pinned A2 is ~⅓ of the isotropic value (CC694B's ~0.02).

**Lifshitz (z=2) re-pin estimate.** One-loop proxy: the induced 4-derivative coefficient
∝ ∫dω d³k /(ω²+A1k²+A3k⁴+m²)². Ratio of the Lifshitz integral (A3≠0) to the relativistic
one (A3=0, cutoff k_UV):

| A3 | k_UV | ratio z2/z1 |
|---|---|---|
| 0.281 | 1.9 | 1.54 |
| 1e-4 | 100 | 0.89 |
| **1e-6 (baryon regime)** | 1000 | **0.66** |

In the baryon regime (tiny A3) the Lifshitz UV **suppresses** the induced Skyrme
coefficient by ≈0.66: B2 ≈ 0.02 → ≈0.013, which gives **frozen E·R ≈ 6–6.5 (≈1.5–1.6×
proton)** — down from ≈9 at the isotropic value. A real, parameter-free improvement
toward the hadronic band, but **not** a complete closure to 4.06 (that needs ≈0.35
suppression).

**Conclusion #5:** the UV pinning of A2 is the genuine lever (smaller B2 → lighter
baryon), and the Lifshitz heat kernel moves it the right way by a derived factor ≈0.66,
bringing the *frozen* (un-melted) S³ baryon to E·R≈6, i.e. ~1.5× the proton, with no
free parameters and no melting.

## Net picture after #4 + #5
- Core-melting is a dead end (collapses or stays heavy; FRG doesn't rescue it).
- The honest, parameter-free path is: correct UV-pinned A2 (Lifshitz ⇒ ×0.66 ⇒
  frozen E·R≈6) **then** collective/quantum corrections (resume #2, typically −20–40%
  on the mass). 6 × (0.6–0.8) ≈ 3.6–4.8 — i.e. the proton is plausibly reached by
  **UV re-pin + quantization**, not by amplitude dressing.
- Caveats (honest): the Lifshitz factor 0.66 is a one-loop *proxy* (ratio of scalar
  loop integrals), not the full anisotropic a₂; the frozen E·R(B2) values are from the
  size-controlled relaxer (degree≈0.95–1.0). Both should be firmed up, but the
  direction and rough magnitude are robust.
