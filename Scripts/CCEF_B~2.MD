# Hypothesis: baryon mass from the CCEF phase transition (no hot Big Bang)

Hypothesis-only exploration, before any tree-level free parameter. Premise: CCEF has no
hot Big Bang — only an ordering phase transition of the order parameter n. If the
transition involves changing/running parameters and a distinct disordered phase, the baryon
mass (E·R) may be set by transition/two-phase physics rather than by present-vacuum
single-phase equilibrium (which gave the heavy E·R≈9–12).

## The idea in one line
The baryon may not be a single-phase soliton in equilibrium with today's vacuum; it may be
a **two-phase object** — a topological winding wrapped around a bubble of the (meta)stable
**disordered** phase — formed at the ordering transition. Its E·R is then governed by
bubble energetics (surface tension + latent heat), which is naturally lower than the
single-phase value.

## CCEF-native mechanisms that could realize this
1. **Kibble formation.** Topological defects (the S³ solitons / flux-ring baryons) form at
   a symmetry-breaking transition via the Kibble mechanism. Their abundance and their
   internal structure are set at formation, when the ordering is incomplete (amplitude
   χ_form < 1, gap Λ still opening, dimensionless couplings still running).
2. **Running dimensionless couplings through the transition.** E·R depends only on
   dimensionless ratios (A2/A1, A4/A1, and the induced-Skyrme log). The induced Skyrme
   coefficient B2 ∼ ln(k_UV/k_sol) is itself a running coupling. If these ran during
   ordering, the configuration selected at formation differs from the present-vacuum one.
3. **Melted core = bubble of the disordered phase.** The "core melting" found earlier
   (χ→small in the core, which lowers E·R to ∼5) is *literally* a region of restored
   symmetry — the disordered phase — inside the baryon. So the dressed baryon is intrinsically
   a two-phase object, and its energetics involve the ordered/disordered free-energy
   difference (latent heat) and the interface tension — i.e. transition physics.
4. **First-order transition ⇒ stabilizing surface tension.** If the CCEF ordering transition
   is **first-order** (a barrier / a metastable disordered phase), a false-vacuum bubble can
   be stabilized by surface tension against collapse — a thin-wall configuration. This is
   exactly the ingredient missing from the earlier free relaxation (which used the minimal
   double-well V=(m_σ²/8)(χ²−1)², effectively second-order/no barrier, and so let the core
   either collapse or stay heavy).

## What it could explain
- **A lower E·R without a tree parameter:** the two-phase/melted baryon sits at the ∼4–5
  E·R found in the core-melting docs, now with a *dynamical reason to exist and be stable*
  (formed at the transition / stabilized by first-order surface tension) rather than imposed
  by hand in a vacuum where it collapsed.
- **Why the present-vacuum equilibrium calc gives the "wrong" heavy value:** that calc
  assumes a single-phase soliton in the fully-ordered vacuum with a no-barrier potential. If
  the true potential is first-order, the relevant object is the two-phase bubble, not the
  single-phase soliton — different physics, lower E·R.
- **BBN reinterpreted as defect formation.** With no hot BB, light-element / matter content
  is not set by thermal freeze-out but by the ordering transition (Kibble defect density,
  correlation length). The "baryon mass needed for BBN" is then the transition-epoch /
  two-phase mass, and there is no separate thermal-BBN constraint forcing the heavy
  single-phase E·R.

## The crucial consistency check the hypothesis must pass — and how
**Reproducibility objection:** protons made *today* (e.g. in collisions) have the same mass
as primordial ones (938 MeV). So the baryon mass cannot be a frozen relic of formation-era
parameters — newly made protons would then differ. 
**Resolution that survives:** the viable version is **not** "frozen relic with old
parameters" but "**present-day stable two-phase object**." Every proton — primordial or
freshly made — is the same two-phase (false-vacuum-core + winding) configuration, stabilized
by present-day first-order-transition surface tension. The transition cosmology explains the
*existence and population* of these objects; their mass is a present, reproducible property.
This keeps proton universality while still lowering E·R via two-phase energetics. (The pure
"running-parameters-frozen-in" version is disfavored by reproducibility.)

## What the hypothesis requires (testable, not done here)
1. **The CCEF effective potential must be first-order** (a barrier / metastable disordered
   χ=0 phase). The minimal quartic V is not; the *full induced* potential (the same
   heat-kernel/FRG machinery used for the graviton) might generate the cubic/non-analytic
   terms that make it first-order. This is checkable.
2. **A stable thin-wall two-phase soliton must exist** in that potential: winding wrapped on
   a disordered-phase bubble, balanced by surface tension. Re-run the relaxer with a
   first-order V (and the bubble seeded), not the double-well — the earlier collapse may be
   an artifact of the second-order potential.
3. **Its E·R must come out ≈4** from the latent heat + surface tension + winding balance,
   parameter-free (the potential's first-order parameters being themselves induced).

## Honest limits
- This does **not** address the LV / UV / photon issue (that is the A3 sector, independent).
- It does **not** yet produce a number — it needs the first-order potential and the two-phase
  relaxation, which are genuine computations (next session).
- It is **not** guaranteed: if the CCEF transition is second-order (no barrier), the
  two-phase bubble isn't stabilized and the hypothesis fails — back to the heavy single-phase
  baryon or the tree-Skyrme fix.
- It is, however, a **parameter-free** route (the first-order structure would be derived,
  not fitted), which is why it's worth checking before adding a tree-level coupling.

## One-line status
Promising, CCEF-native, reproducibility-safe in its two-phase form, and the cleanest
no-new-parameter alternative — but it stands or falls on whether the induced CCEF potential
is **first-order**. That single question is the decisive next computation.
