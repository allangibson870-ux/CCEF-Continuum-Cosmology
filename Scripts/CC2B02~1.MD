# First-principles anisotropic a₂: overall scale, mixing, and the decisive uncertainty

Full anisotropic (z=2) heat-kernel computation of the induced 4-derivative coefficient,
to pin the overall prefactor g and the S²:TrT² mixing from first principles, then re-run
the soliton. Result: **the answer is controlled by one number — the log IR-matching in
a₂ — which is uncertain by a factor of ~3–6, and the proton sits at its favorable edge.**

## 1. Overall coefficient (X² algebra) and the anisotropy
The a₂ X²-term gives, with K4 = (2/3)S²+(1/3)TrT2, an overall coefficient B2 = I where
I = ∫dω d³k/(2π)⁴ /(ω²+A1k²+A3k⁴+m²)² (= ∫ds·s·K(s), full anisotropic measure included).

| | value |
|---|---|
| I_z2 (Lifshitz, full 4D log) | **0.072** |
| I_z1 (relativistic, cutoff k_UV) | 0.076 |
| anisotropy ratio z2/z1 | **0.949** |

So the **anisotropy is a 5% effect** (confirmed again). But the *absolute* value 0.072 is
**3.6× larger than CC694B's quoted 0.02.** That entire gap is the **log / IR-matching
convention** — where the IR end of ln(k_UV/k_IR) is placed (the gap m=1.886, the soliton
scale k_sol≈2.7, or the full UV→m log). This convention, not the anisotropy, sets B2.

## 2. Mixing ratio S²:TrT²
The 2:1 ratio = (α+2β):(α−2β) with α (X²) and β (Ω²) heat-kernel coefficients. Both the
target-geometry structure (TrX²=S²+TrT2, TrΩ²=2(S²−TrT2)) and the Gilkey tensor weights
(α:β = 1/2:1/12) are anisotropy-independent at leading order, so the ratio stays ≈**2:1**;
the anisotropy is carried by the overall measure (the 0.949). Operator-structure variation
(2:1 → 1:2 → pure Skyrme) shifts E·R by ~30–50% — a secondary lever. (The exact anisotropic
β/α from the spatial-field-strength polarization was *not* computed from first principles
here — flagged as the one remaining piece of the mixing.)

## Verdict
The first-principles anisotropic a₂ **does not uniquely land at 0.011–0.013.** It gives:
- overall scale uncertain by ~3–6× (log/IR-matching) — the band B2 ≈ 0.012–0.072;
- anisotropy correction ≈ −5%;
- mixing ≈ 2:1 (geometry-robust), with ~30–50% structure leverage;
- and the **proton (E·R_baryon=4.06) sits at the LOW/favorable edge (B2≈0.012)** — a
  factor ~1.7 below CC694B's 0.02 and ~6 below the naive full-log value.

So the classical proton is **reachable but not robustly predicted**: it requires the a₂
log IR-matching to land at the soft end *and* the charge-radius convention *and* a
lighter-leaning operator structure. None of these is excluded; none is yet derived
convention-free.

**The single decisive open computation** is now sharp: a convention-free determination of
the IR-matching in the induced a₂ (i.e. fix the log's lower limit by properly matching the
one-loop effective action onto the soliton scale, not by hand). That one number decides
whether B2 is ~0.012 (proton) or ~0.04 (3× too heavy). It is a genuine calculation, not a
quick check.

## Standing of the program
- Gravity/cosmology spine: solid (massless, healthy, z=1 composite graviton).
- Matter sector: the ~2× "too heavy" verdict softens to **"undetermined within a factor
  ~3 normalization window, with the proton at the favorable edge."** Quantization adds
  ~+9% (headwind); the uncomputed Casimir (negative) could offset it. Honest status:
  neither confirmed nor excluded, pending the a₂ IR-matching.

## Caveats
- B2=I via the X²-term algebra; the absolute identification carries the same a₂
  normalization convention under discussion (that *is* the result).
- Anisotropic β/α (mixing correction) not computed from first principles — leading-order
  2:1 assumed from geometry; structure scan bounds its E·R impact at ~30–50%.
- E·R uses baryon/charge rms (the apples-to-apples match to m_p·r_charge/ℏc); energy-rms
  values run ~30% higher.
