# Is the induced CCEF potential first-order? — No, it's second-order

Decisive test for the phase-transition baryon hypothesis (which needed a first-order
transition to stabilize a two-phase / false-vacuum-core baryon). Computed the one-loop
(Coleman-Weinberg) effective potential for the order-parameter amplitude χ, with the
emergent CP¹ gauge field as the candidate first-order driver (Halperin-Lubensky-Ma /
Coleman-Weinberg mechanism).

## Method
The ordered phase Higgses the emergent U(1) (CP¹), so ordering is scalar-QED-like. Built
V_eff(χ) = tree (r/2)χ² + (λ/4)χ⁴ + one-loop CW from the gauge field (mass M_A²=e_γ²χ²,
3 polarizations) and the scalar modes, with the vacuum fixed at χ=1. Looked for a barrier
(a local maximum between χ=0 and χ=1, with χ=0 a metastable local minimum).

## Result: NO barrier — second-order
With CCEF's couplings, V_eff(χ)−V_eff(0) decreases **monotonically** from χ=0 to the
vacuum at χ=1 (no bump, no metastable χ=0). The transition is **second-order**.

The reason is quantitative:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| emergent gauge coupling e_γ² = 1/4A2 | 0.765 |
| gauge CW coefficient 3e_γ⁴/64π² | 0.0028 |
| tree quartic λ/4 | 0.111 |
| ratio (gauge CW)/(tree quartic) | **0.025** |
| e_γ² needed for a barrier | ~4.8 |
| ⇒ CCEF is ~6× too weakly coupled (and CW coeff ~40× too small) |

The gauge contribution is loop-suppressed (the 1/64π²) and the emergent coupling e_γ²≈0.77
is far too small for the gauge fluctuations to overcome the tree quartic and carve a
barrier. The scalar modes (σ, π) are the same loop size and don't help.

## The irony: no hot Big Bang removes the usual first-order driver
The mechanism that *robustly* makes ordering transitions first-order is the
**finite-temperature cubic** term, −(T/12π)Σ M³(χ), from thermal boson loops. **CCEF has no
hot Big Bang — no temperature — so this term is absent.** The very feature that motivated
the hypothesis (phase transition instead of hot BB) also removes the standard route to
first-order. What remains is the zero-T Coleman-Weinberg route, which here is far too weak.

## Verdict and consequence
- **The induced CCEF potential is second-order.** No metastable disordered phase, no
  surface-tension stabilization, no thin-wall two-phase bubble.
- Therefore the **phase-transition / two-phase baryon hypothesis is not supported**: the
  earlier core-melting collapse was not an artifact of a wrong (second-order) potential —
  the potential really is second-order, so the melted-core baryon genuinely does not
  stabilize by transition physics.
- This route does **not** rescue the baryon mass. The honest options revert to:
  (a) add a fundamental tree-level Lorentz-invariant Skyrme coupling (~0.012) — one free
  parameter, decouples baryon from the UV scale; or
  (b) accept the baryon as a ~4× near-miss (high-scale safe regulator, induced Skyrme).

## Caveats
- e_γ²=1/4A2 (the exact CCEF magnetic-coupling identity); the conclusion is robust to O(1)
  shifts since first-order needs a ~6× larger coupling.
- CW computed in the relativistic limit for the *shape* (valid: field-dependent masses
  M(χ)~O(1) ≪ k_UV, so the barrier-determining loop momenta are in the z=1 region; the
  Lifshitz A3 affects only the UV/renormalization, not the barrier).
- A genuinely first-order transition would require either a much larger emergent gauge
  coupling, a fundamental cubic (symmetry-forbidden for O(N)), or some non-thermal cubic
  source not present in the current CCEF action.
